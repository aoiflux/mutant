# Case QUILLDROP — the brief

**Read this to the room. Then open [01_scene.mut](01_scene.mut).**

---

Meridian Logistics moves freight. Four hundred people, one finance team, one
quarterly close that has to be filed on the 15th.

On **12 March 2026** finance cannot open the close. The files are where they
should be. They are the right size. They open to garbage.

IT rebuilt the machine before anyone thought to image it. What you have is one
triage collection from `MRD-FIN-07`, an accounts-payable workstation belonging
to **r. mehta** — a folder of files, and a bodyfile giving four timestamps for
every object that was ever on it, including the ones that are not there any
more.

No memory capture. No packet capture. No disk image. One folder and a list of
times.

That is enough.

---

## What you are being asked

Five questions, in the order a real examination asks them:

1. **What have we got?** — and what is wrong with it. (step 1)
2. **How did it start?** — the mail, the download, the thing that ran. (steps 2–4)
3. **What happened, when?** — the day in order. (step 5)
4. **What is a lie?** — the timestamps that cannot all be true. (steps 6–7)
5. **Can you prove any of it tomorrow?** — the handover. (steps 8–10)

## Three things worth saying out loud

1. **You cannot delete a fact. You can only create a contradiction.**
   The staging folder is gone from the disk. It is still in the timeline. A
   file whose content-modified time precedes its own creation time is not
   evidence of a date — it is evidence of an edit.

2. **The attacker wrote you an inventory list and called it a zip.**
   A zip's central directory names every member, with its size and its CRC-32,
   before you decompress anything. Step 7 never decompresses a byte and still
   tells you exactly what left.

3. **Analysis is what you did. Evidence is what you can prove you did.**
   Step 9 seals the case. Step 10 checks the seal. Change one word of the
   report between them and watch which number moves and which one does not.

## Where the evidence comes from

Every byte is synthetic, generated by [`fsagen`](https://github.com/aoiflux/fsagen)
from `--seed 88412`. Nothing here is real and nothing is malicious: the
executables are structurally valid PE files with no code in them.

**What is reproducible, and what is not — this distinction is the workshop's
last lesson.** The corpus is byte-identical on every laptop in the room, so the
three exhibit digests step 9 prints are the same for everybody. The *manifest*
step 9 writes is not, and must not be: it records when this examination was
opened, when each exhibit was registered, and when the seal was signed. Two
examinations are two events. Reproducible evidence, singular custody — both are
correct, and a tool that gave you one number for both would be lying about one
of them.
